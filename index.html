TeacherCollab

Practicing responsive teaching with simulated AI colleagues

Stanford University

Teachers examine authentic student work and discuss the evidence with AI-simulated teacher colleagues. The teacher remains responsible for interpreting student thinking and deciding how to respond. TeacherCollab is designed to adapt across teaching contexts. So far, we have developed versions for mathematics and computer science.

Project overview Live prototype

A practice space grounded in student thinking

Teachers begin with an artifact, bring multiple perspectives into view, and remain responsible for the instructional judgment.

  1. 01

    Examine the evidence

    Compare student work, explanations, dialogue, or classroom video before deciding what the learner understands.

  2. 02

    Invite partial perspectives

    Choose one AI colleague for focused sense-making or several colleagues for a roundtable.

  3. 03

    Interpret and respond

    Question, compare, or reject the colleagues’ contributions, then articulate a defensible next move.

What we are learning

TeacherCollab is an ongoing research and design project. The evidence to date guides our next iteration. It does not yet show changes in teaching practice or student learning.

The value of multiple AI colleagues is not yet demonstrated

In two short CS sessions, both participants were positive about the prototype, but only one explicitly valued consulting several AI personas. In a separate session with 12 early-career mathematics teachers, the AI colleagues often felt indistinguishable and were not consistently brought into the conversation.

The teacher must remain the decision-maker

Participants sometimes experienced the AI colleagues as experts or coaches rather than collaborators. The next iteration will make each AI colleague’s purpose and limits clearer while keeping the teacher responsible for interpreting the evidence and choosing a response.

What comes next

We will revise the system with university section leaders, test how one-on-one and roundtable formats fit existing professional learning, and study how teachers interpret, challenge, and synthesize the colleagues’ contributions.
